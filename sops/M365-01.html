1. Purpose

This is the reference document. It says what Microsoft 365 services the organization provides, to whom, in what configuration, and where each thing belongs — so that the rest of the catalogue can say “per M365-01” instead of re-explaining.

It exists because a ticket like “App & email access — FAQs from staff” tends to sit past its service target. A set of basic questions ages into a breach when there is nowhere to point. Appendix A is written to be that place.

3. What is supported, and what is tolerated

Three tiers of support, stated honestly

SUPPORTED — IT will help, and will fix it. The configurations below.

TOLERATED — it will probably work, and IT will help on a best-effort basis, but will not spend a long time on it and may ask you to move to a supported configuration.

NOT SUPPORTED — IT cannot help. Not unwillingness; genuinely no ability to diagnose or fix.

Saying which is which is kinder than discovering it during a problem, and it lets Tier 1 answer confidently instead of hedging.

5. What the organization does not provide

Stated so that nobody spends time looking for it.

  • Personal Microsoft accounts, or personal use of the organization tenant.
  • Mailboxes for people without a relationship with the organization. A guest is not a mailbox holder (ACC-10 §5).
  • Unlimited retention. Content is retained per policy (M365-16), not forever by default.
  • Support for personally-purchased software, whatever it is used for.
  • Data recovery from a personally-owned device.
  • Copilot or other generative AI as a provisioned organization service, at present (M365-18).

6. Availability and support hours

  • The Microsoft 365 service itself is provided by Microsoft with its own availability commitment. IT does not control it and cannot restore it during a Microsoft outage — what IT can do is tell the organization what is happening, which it will.
  • IT support hours are per GOV-06 §6, by tier. Those figures need confirming from the Head of IT before this document is published.
  • Where Tier 1 coverage reduces during holiday periods, that is published in advance rather than discovered on the day.
  • Where a Microsoft service is degraded, IT posts to the knowledge base rather than waiting to be asked. A community that can see the status stops opening tickets about it.

7. Where to go for what

Something is broken The service desk — the front door for everything technology-related (GOV-02 §3).
You need access to something The service desk. The owner of the thing approves; IT does the granting (M365-11).
You want to know how to do something The knowledge base first, then the service desk.
It is not IT’s at all The service desk will route it to the right desk, and that counts as it being dealt with.
You think an account has been compromised The service desk immediately, saying so explicitly (M365-22).
You have a suggestion about a procedure Raise it against the document ID through the service desk (GOV-01 §10).

Appendix A — Staff FAQ

Publish this on the knowledge base under GOV-05, and use it to close the FAQ ticket.

Question Answer
What email do I get? A full Exchange Online mailbox with an address at your domain, reachable from Outlook on your computer, at outlook.office.com, and on your phone.
Can I read organization email on my own phone? Yes. We apply protection to the organization apps only — see DEV-07 for exactly what we can and cannot see, which is much less than people assume.
Where should I save my files? Your own drafts and working notes: OneDrive. Anything your department relies on, or that the organization would need if you left: your department’s SharePoint site.
Do I need the VPN? Almost certainly not. Email, Teams, SharePoint and OneDrive all work from anywhere. The VPN is only for on-premises systems (DEV-10).
I forgot my password aka.ms/sspr resets it without contacting anyone, provided you have two security methods registered. Registering the second one takes two minutes and is the best five minutes you will spend.
I have a new phone Set up your security method on the new phone BEFORE you get rid of the old one. Otherwise you will need to verify your identity in person or on video with photo ID.
Which browser should I use? Current Edge, Chrome, Firefox or Safari. All fine.
Can I use a different mail app? Outlook is what we support. Others may work, but we will not be able to help much when they do not.
How do I get a shared departmental mailbox? Ask the service desk. It needs a named owner from your department who approves who can read it (M365-03).
How do I email everyone in a group? Through the office that owns that population — usually HR or Communications (M365-06).
How do I get a SharePoint site? Ask the service desk with a named owner, a one-sentence purpose, and who needs access (M365-10).
Something is broken. Who do I ask? The service desk. If it turns out not to be ours, we will send it to the right desk.
I think somebody else has been in my account Tell the service desk straight away and say so explicitly. Do not change your password first — we need to see the sign-in trail before it changes.
Will IT ever ask for my password? Never. Not by email, phone or ticket. Anyone who does is not IT.
