## 1.  Purpose

Most organizations have an email and account policy, and it governs. This standard is the operational layer underneath: the numbers, the defaults, and the handful of rules that IT applies consistently.

## 2.  What every mailbox gets

## 3.  External forwarding — the one hard rule

> **Automatic forwarding of work mail to an external address is blocked, for everybody.**
> This is the single most valuable mailbox control the organization can operate, and it is worth the small amount of annoyance it causes.
> Two reasons. First, it is the standard exfiltration mechanism after an account compromise: an attacker sets a forwarding rule and reads everything thereafter without signing in again, which is invisible to the account holder and survives a password reset. Second, work correspondence forwarded to a personal account is outside every retention, discovery and security control the organization operates — and it is frequently correspondence about other people.
> Manual forwarding of an individual message remains available. That is a person making a decision about a specific message, which is a different thing entirely.

Where a genuine business need exists — a shared function forwarding to a monitored departmental address inside the tenant — that is internal forwarding and is fine. External forwarding exceptions require Tier 4 approval, a stated reason, and an expiry, and should be very rare.

## 4.  Rules and automatic processing

- Users may create their own inbox rules for filing and organisation. That is ordinary use and needs no involvement from IT.
- Rules that delete, or that move mail out of the mailbox, are the attacker’s pattern (M365-22 §5). IT reports on newly-created rules of that shape and follows up — not to police filing habits, but because a rule the user did not create is a compromise indicator.
- When investigating a suspected compromise, checking for attacker-created rules is mandatory and is the step most often missed.
- Automatic replies should say when the person is back and who to contact meanwhile. An out-of-office giving neither is a small daily cost across the whole organization.
## 5.  Delegation and shared access

| Mechanism | Use when |
|---|---|
| Delegate access | One person manages another’s mail or calendar — an assistant, or cover during leave. Personal and revocable (ACC-14). |
| Shared mailbox membership | Several people answer mail to a departmental address. This is the right answer far more often than delegation (M365-03). |
| Send-as | Mail should appear to come from the department rather than the individual. |
| Calendar sharing | Availability visible to colleagues. Default is free/busy; detail by choice. |
| Full mailbox access granted by IT without the owner’s knowledge | Never, except under GOV-03 or a Tier 4 decision with a recorded reason. |

## 6.  Sending limits and moderation

- Platform recipient and rate limits apply and are not adjustable. Where somebody hits them, the answer is usually that they should be using M365-06 rather than sending manually.
- Whole-population groups are moderated (M365-04 §8). One misdirected reply-all to the whole organization is a genuinely bad afternoon.
- Bulk sending for legitimate organization communication goes through the approved mechanism, not through a personal mailbox.
## 7.  Retention and archiving

- Retention policy is applied at tenant level per M365-16, not chosen per mailbox.
- The archive is part of the mailbox, not a backup. Deleting from the archive deletes it.
- On separation, mailbox content is retained a minimum of two years with no maximum (ACC-01 §7.3). Confirm that Teams chat retention meets the same floor, or the floor is defeated for one content type.
- Individual users do not need to manage retention. Where somebody asks how long their mail is kept, the answer is in M365-16 and it should be a published answer rather than a per-question one.
## 8.  Mailboxes that are not people

| Type | Governed by |
|---|---|
| Shared mailbox for a department or function | M365-03. Named owner, membership, review. |
| Room and equipment mailboxes | Booking policy set by the room’s owning department; IT provisions. |
| Service or application mailbox | ACC-08. Must have a human owner recorded. |
| A mailbox for a departed person | Converted to shared or retained inactive per ACC-01 §7.3. Never left as a live licensed account with somebody else reading it. |

## Appendix A — Desk card

|  | They ask | You say |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | “Can I forward my work mail to Gmail?” | No — external forwarding is blocked for everyone. You can forward individual messages by hand. If it is a workload problem, a shared mailbox may be the answer. |
| 2 | “My mailbox is full” | Check the archive is enabled and the licence is right before anything else. |
| 3 | “Can somebody manage my calendar?” | Yes — delegation. We need your consent on the ticket. |
| 4 | “Several of us need to answer this address” | That is a shared mailbox, not delegation (M365-03). It needs a named owner. |
| 5 | “How long is my email kept?” | Per the retention policy — M365-16 has the published answer. |
| 6 | “There is a rule in my mailbox I did not create” | Stop. That is a compromise indicator. M365-22 §5, same day. |
| 7 | “I need to email everyone” | M365-06. Through the office that owns that population. |
